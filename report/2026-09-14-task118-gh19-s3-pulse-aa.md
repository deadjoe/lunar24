## 1. The candidate

One candidate is proposed, `C1`, entirely inside the existing production render path.

```
corrected_pulse(t) = naive(t) + R(t, w) - R(frac(t - duty), w)            // dt = f0 / sr
                                                                          // w  = min(dt, 0.5)
```

`w` is the **capped kernel width** (revision 4, §12): for `dt <= 0.5` it IS `dt`, bit for bit, so the
in-domain formula is the one above it; past `0.5` the width is held at `0.5` while the phase
accumulator goes on advancing by the true `dt`. That is the only thing revision 4 changed, and it
changed nothing else — not the frequency, not the CV law, not the duty, not the PWM transfer.

`R` is the repository's existing polyBLEP residual (`core/include/lunar24/core/polyblep_kernel.h`,
landed in S2/task #110) — reused, not re-derived. Edge A sits at phase 0 and is a **+2** jump so its
residual is **added**; edge B sits at phase `duty` and is a **−2** jump so its residual is
**subtracted**. The sign rule is not a convention: `R(0+) = -1` and `R(1-) = +1`, so a downward step
needs the residual subtracted to move both one-sided limits onto a common value. S2's saw correction
is a single-edge instance of the same rule; a pulse is **two** edges per period, which is the whole
content of the difference and is why reusing the saw correction verbatim is wrong.

Files: new `core/include/lunar24/core/pulse_blep_kernel.h`; `core/include/lunar24/core/vco.h`
(`emittedAt_` extended, `pulseBlepCorr_`/`pulseBlepWeight_` added);
`core/include/lunar24/core/vco_wave_map.h` (`pulseWeight` added). The correction enters
`emittedAt_`, which is what `tick()` writes to the output and what sizes the hard-sync jump, so this
is production sampling, not an analyzer-side rewrite.

### 1.1 Support overlap: no guard, and why the guard was the defect

A square has two edges, so the two correction windows are disjoint **iff**
`min(duty, 1-duty) >= 2*dt`; when they are not, the correction terms overlap in phase. Revision 1
turned that fact into a **duty-dependent switch**: outside the disjoint domain
`polyblepPulseCorrection` returned exactly `0.0` and the cell rendered as the naive pulse.

**That switch was a defect, and it is removed.** @Codex compiled the header at
`dt = 220/44100`, `phase = dt/4`, `duty = 2*dt ± 1e-12` and measured the output stepping from `1` to
`0.4375` — a jump of **0.5625** at a phase no pulse edge passes through. The switch was correcting
each edge in isolation and then abandoning the correction entirely the moment the windows touched,
so crossing `2*dt` moved a sample that nothing about the waveform had moved. A legal PWM trajectory
passes through that duty, so the discontinuity was reachable by the product.

```
REPRO duty=2dt-1e-12  guarded=1.000000000000000  current=0.437500000000000  disjoint=0
REPRO duty=2dt+1e-12  guarded=0.437500000000000  current=0.437500000000000  disjoint=1
REPRO jump guarded=0.562500000000000   current=0.000000000000000
```

`polyblepPulseWindowsDisjoint` still exists and still computes the same predicate, but it is now
**reporting only with no callers**: the probe prints it per cell so the boundary arithmetic stays
auditable. Window non-overlap is a *sufficient* condition for each term's phase-local derivation; it
is not a necessary one, and it is not a statement that a narrow pulse has no band-limited
representation. The header says so where the function is defined.

### 1.2 The removed guard's three jobs, verified in the header directly

`tools/gh19_s3_pulse_kernel_sweep.cpp` compiles the header with no product link and sweeps
`(dt, duty, t)`; `tools/run_gh19_s3_kernel_sweep.py` drives it and fails closed if the driver's own
positive control — the refuted formula's 0.5625 jump — is not reproduced. A build failure or a
non-zero probe exit is a **tool failure**, not a measurement, and is reported as such.

* **The boundary is continuous now.** At the review's exact coordinate, refining the duty grid by
  10x from 1e3 to 1e6 points: the *refuted* formula's max gap **stays at 0.562500000000000** at every
  refinement (a real discontinuity does not shrink), while the current header's max gap is
  **0.000000000000000** at every refinement.
* **Elsewhere it is only the pulse's own edges.** At `duty = 0.4` — a legal PWM crossing, far from
  `2*dt` — both formulas show a max gap that falls 10x per 10x refinement
  (`0.0786 → 0.0080 → 0.00080 → 0.000080`), i.e. a converging edge, not a jump.
* **Bounded.** Over a 64 x 1500 x 1500 `(dt, duty, t)` grid plus the product's own duty clamp
  `[1e-3, 1-1e-3]`, `max |out| = 1.000000000000000`, excess over 1 = **0.000e+00**. The duty→0 limit
  is `-1`.
* **Narrow pulse.** Swept over `duty in [1e-3, 4*dt]`: `max |out| = 1.000000000000000`, with the
  range table showing the transition from the degenerate `duty = 0` limit (`[-1, -1]`) up to full
  swing.
* **Moving duty.** For `dt = 220/44100` and `dt = 5000/44100`, sweeping 512 phases and duty from
  0.001 to 0.95, with the duty grid refined in 4x steps (400, 100, 25, 6.25, 1.5625, 0.390625,
  0.09765625, 0.02441406 windows per frame):

  | duty grid | 400 | 100 | 25 | 6.25 | 1.5625 | 0.390625 | 0.0977 | 0.0244 |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | refuted | 0.000 | 2.000 | 2.000 | 2.000 | **2.000** | **2.000** | **2.000** | **2.000** |
  | current | 0.000 | 2.000 | 2.000 | 2.000 | 1.904 | 0.705 | 0.191 | 0.0485 |

  Both sit at 2.0 while the grid is too coarse to place a sample inside the pulse's own edge — that
  2.0 is the real edge traversed at full swing between two adjacent grid points, and it is not the
  thing under test. The distinguishing behaviour is what happens **after** the grid can resolve an
  edge: the refuted formula **never leaves 2.0** (a jump does not shrink with refinement, at any
  resolution down to 0.024 windows/frame), while the current header falls by roughly 8x per 8x
  refinement. A step that converges with refinement is a sampled edge; one that does not is a
  discontinuity. The `dt = 5000/44100` sweep behaves the same way.

Measured, the removal also *improves* the three cells the switch used to disable — see §2.

## 2. Static matrix (axis 1 of 2)

Route: analytic Fourier reference, truncated at the last harmonic below Nyquist (`kmax` per cell), so
there is no leakage floor to mistake for a result. The metric is **总诊断残差**, dB relative to the
reference's own RMS, with the phase **pinned**, never fitted; only the scale `A` is fitted and `A`,
`fund` and `dc` are printed per cell.

80 pure-pulse cells: 4 sample rates (44.1/48/88.2/96 kHz) x {220, 440, 880, 3520, 5000} Hz x
pulse widths {.1, .5, .9}, side A (36 cells `vco_a_pulse_*`, 8 `vco_a_pulsehi_*`) and side B (36
`vco_b_pulse_*`).

* **80 of 80 improved by more than 0.50 dB; median -7.55 dB, range -4.71..-9.50 dB.**
  Revision 1 reported 77/80 with three cells at exactly +0.00 dB, because those three were the
  guard's disabled cells. With the guard gone they improve like everything else
  (`vco_a_pulsehi_44100_3520_pw10`: -12.35 → -17.06 dB, i.e. **-4.71 dB**), which is the removal's
  own independent confirmation on product-rendered cells.
* **No improvement is bought by level or DC.** Across all 80 cells the fitted quantities move by
  `max |Δdc| = 0.00127`, `max |Δfund| = 0.0265`, `max |ΔA| = 0.0351`.
* **The duty is not traded — by construction, not by measurement.** The `duty` column of the matrix
  is the cell's **declared** parameter (`c.duty_param`), identical in both arms because it is an
  input, so a zero delta there would prove nothing and **is not quoted as evidence**. The structural
  reason is in §1: the correction is *added to* `naive(t)` and never enters the phase comparison
  `phi < duty`, so every edge stays exactly where the naive pulse puts it, and both residual terms are
  non-zero only within `dt` of an edge — which is also why the boundedness grid in §1.2 holds. What
  the self-check does measure is how far the residual would move for a *real* duty error, and that is
  **not arm-independent**: a `1e-3` duty error costs **+0.817 dB** on the baseline frame and
  **+2.718 dB** on the candidate frame (both `WORSE`, both `ok`). So the residual column alone could
  not exclude a sub-`1e-3` duty shift; the structural argument is what covers that gap, and it is
  stated rather than left implicit.
* Both extremes of the fitted columns sit on the **highest-frequency cells**. `max |ΔA|` (0.0351,
  ~7% of `A ≈ 0.5`) and `max |Δfund|` (0.0265) land on the **same** cell,
  `vco_a_pulsehi_44100_5000_pw50`, where only `kmax = 4` harmonics fit below Nyquist; the rest of both
  top-four lists is the rest of the 5000 Hz band and `vco_a_pulsehi_44100_3520_pw10`
  (`kmax = 6`). At those coordinates the corrected edge reshapes the few components the reference
  actually has, so part of those cells' improvement is a change in the fundamental's amplitude rather
  than in removed alias energy. Named here rather than averaged away; these are the cells where the
  static column is weakest. The `kmax = 4` cell also carries the *largest* improvement (-9.50 dB),
  which is exactly why it is flagged rather than celebrated.

### 2.1 What the floor/harmonic split does and does not show

The residual is split into two **linear power shares**: `harm_db = 10*log10(harm/tot)` and
`floor_db = 10*log10(floor/tot)`. Two consequences have to be stated plainly, and revision 1 got
both wrong.

* **They are shares, so the linear powers add to 1 — not the dB values to 0.** The printed columns
  satisfy `10^(floor_db/10) + 10^(harm_db/10) = 1` to the printed precision (max deviation
  `9.7e-4` over the 80 baseline cells, which is just the two-decimal rounding). For
  `vco_a_pulse_44100_220_pw10` the two values are `-0.04` and `-20.30`, summing to `-20.34` dB.
  Revision 1 said they "sum to 0 dB by construction"; that is refuted by this report's own numbers.
* **The partition cannot identify aliases.** `k = round(f/f0)` classifies by frequency
  NEIGHBOURHOOD, so a folded image that lands inside the guard of a legitimate harmonic is counted
  as a harmonic. At 48 kHz / 220 Hz the image that folds onto 220 Hz is counted as the fundamental.
  So the split *bounds how much of the residual sits away from the harmonic comb*; it does not
  separate "alias" from "harmonic error". The measured movement is floor down by
  **-4.98..-10.32 dB** with the harmonic share up correspondingly, which is consistent with removed
  alias energy but is **not sufficient evidence of it**. The one place this slice does measure
  aliasing directly is the total residual, which falls on every cell.

## 3. Mixed waveform (S6 baseline)

6 `vco_a_ringmix_*` cells at morph 0.8125 / 0.875 / 0.9375, i.e. pulse-node weight `u` = .25/.5/.75 on
stretch 3 of the morph ring, two sample rates. The reference is built for the mix —
`(1-u)*BL_triangle + u*BL_pulse` — because the triangle node is corrected by BLAMP and the pulse node
by BLEP, and each must be band-limited in its own right. The triangle series is derived from
`triangleShape`'s own closed form and verified numerically against the naive shape (max deviation
`3e-8` away from corners at `kmax=400`, DC `-6e-17`); the self-check confirms `u=1` reproduces the
pure-pulse reference **exactly** and `u=0` converges to the band-limited triangle inside its analytic
tail bound.

**All 6 cells improve, -7.49..-7.54 dB**, with the floor share down 8.46..8.82 dB and
`max |ΔA| = 0.0029`, `max |Δfund| = 0.0002`, `max |Δdc| = 0.00004` (the duty is a declared input here
too — see §2). These are the S6 baseline coordinates; the residual in
this column is **not comparable** to the pure-pulse column — it is a mixed waveform with its own
reference, and the report says so where the column is printed.

## 4. Dynamic PWM matrix (axis 2 of 2)

Route: high-rate **frozen-duty-per-frame** model + polyphase FIR decimation, group delay removed
exactly and integrally. Oversampling `L=8`, 2001 taps, 8 referenced moving-duty cells, 3 of them
convergence-checked against an independently re-designed `2L` decimator.

The cable is a **fact of the DeviceState** on the primary arm (`inputCable[sink]` / `cableSource[sink]`)
and the render travels `make_default_device_state -> encode_device_state -> decode_device_state ->
owner.applyDeviceState` (`tests/host/test_engine_harness.h:59-66`), so the restore path is the one
being measured, not a post-load graph edit. The companion `*_viaConnect` cells reach the same routing
through `connect()` + `rebuild()` and are a **control**, not a substitute: a difference between a
cell and its companion means the saved patch and the patched one are not one signal.

* **All 8 referenced cells improve, -6.77..-7.48 dB** (median -7.36).
* Reference convergence on the three checked cells: `refconv_rms_db` **-43.70..-43.77 dB** against
  residuals of **-30.95..-31.20 dB**, so the reference's own movement sits **12.6 dB below** the
  residual it is used to measure. In revision 1 this column read -37.65..-37.73 dB; the difference is
  a **units defect, now fixed** — see §4.1. `drefconv_db` is `+0.00 dB` on every cell. Note *why* it
  is exactly zero rather than merely close: the column is `rms(A*(r-r2))` over `rms(A*r)`, so the
  fitted `A` cancels and the figure is **arm-independent by construction** — it is computed from the
  two reference models and never touches a rendered sample. That makes it a statement about the
  reference, not an independent control on the candidate, and it is reported as the former.
* Each cell's manifest row carries the measured `f0` and the model's starting phase `phi0_model`
  under the `n+1` convention (`tick()` advances `cumPitch_` and then emits, so the sample at absolute
  frame `n` has phase `frac((n+1)*f0/sr)`), and each dynamic cell ships its per-frame
  `lfo_a_cv_out` trace.
* **Square-LFO cells are excluded from every improvement claim** in this report and in the analyzer's

  precisely in §4.2 — not smoothing.
* The A/B non-symmetry control: on B cells the default graph feeds `vco_b.cv_in` from
  `vco_a.dry_out`, so `cv_amt = 0` is set on the B arm to isolate the B measurement from that route,
  and a B-side dynamic cell is rendered through the same state-carried cable (`vco_b_dynpwm_*`).

### 4.1 The reference's units, and the decimator's real response

Two documentation/implementation mismatches @Codex found, both now measured rather than asserted.

**(a) The convergence column was in the wrong units.** The residual is `x - A*r` and `ref_rms` is
`rms(A*r)`, so both are device-scale; the raw reference movement `r - r2` is in *reference* units.
Comparing them directly understates the movement by exactly `20*log10|A|` — about **6 dB** at the
fitted `A ≈ 0.5`, which is the one thing the column exists to get right. The movement is now scaled
by the same fitted `A`, and the analyzer prints a line above the column header saying so. The change
moved the figure from -37.7 dB to -43.7 dB, i.e. 6.02 dB — the predicted amount, which is itself the
evidence that the diagnosis was right.

**(b) The decimator's docstring described a filter that does not exist.** It claimed the cutoff sat at
the output Nyquist *with the transition band above it*, so the passband stayed flat up to `sr/2`. A
windowed sinc with `fc` at Nyquist is necessarily **-6.0206 dB there**, and `--self-check` now
measures the response and asserts the corrected claims (`|H|` in dB at an output-band frequency,
evaluated with an incremental phasor; the full block is in `c1_matrix.txt`):

```
|H| at the output Nyquist            -6.0206 dB   AS DOCUMENTED (ok)
passband 100-5000 Hz @ 44100 Hz    worst |dev| = 0.000000 dB at 4768 Hz  FLAT (ok)
passband 100-5000 Hz @ 96000 Hz    worst |dev| = 0.000000 dB at 4980 Hz  FLAT (ok)
effective full band (|dev| <= 0.1 dB)  0.4919 cycles/out = 98.38% of the output Nyquist (21692 Hz @44.1k)
transition band, sampled:
  f_out=0.4900   -0.0086 dB | 0.4999  -5.8401 dB | 0.5000  -6.0206 dB
  f_out=0.5001   -6.2049 dB | 0.5020 -10.5010 dB | 0.5100 -60.0903 dB
DC gain |H(0)| (normalized to 1)   -0.000000000 dB  UNITY (ok)
0.1 dB endpoint, convergence   0.4681 (501 taps) <= 0.4919 (2001 taps) < 0.4956 (4001 taps)  CONVERGING (ok)
```

So the rolloff **straddles** the output Nyquist, not sits above it: -0.1 dB is reached at 0.4919
cycles/out (98.4% of Nyquist), -6 dB essentially *at* Nyquist, -60 dB just above it. The passband,
transition-band and convergence questions are now answered separately, the scale is pinned by
`|H(0)| = 1`, and convergence is asserted in taps (a longer filter must move the 0.1 dB endpoint
toward Nyquist) rather than assumed — the earlier "the filter is too short" diagnosis was falsified
by measurement and this check is what would have caught it. `beta` is **dead**: three call sites pass
`0.06` and no constant derived from it appears in the function, so the check asserts that two
different betas give byte-identical coefficients. The day someone wires `beta` up, that goes red
instead of the parameter silently acquiring meaning.

### 4.2 What the dynamic reference is, exactly

`duty_of_frame(floor(t))` makes the duty a piecewise-constant function of time with jumps at
output-frame boundaries — a ZOH of the control parameter. Two things this does **not** do, both of
which revision 1 asserted: it does **not** turn a step into a ramp, and it does **not** lock the
oscillator's threshold crossing to the output frame grid. The crossing is still where the
continuously-advancing phase meets the held duty, so its *time* is resolved at `1/L` of an output
frame and refining `L` does refine edge placement. What `L` cannot refine is the duty trajectory's
own quantization to control-rate frames.

So the reference is the band-limited ideal of **the product's own per-frame duty sampling** — a
*frozen-duty-per-frame approximation* of a continuously moving edge — and is not the band-limited
ideal of a continuously-moving edge. Whether a smoothly-moving PWM edge is representable at all is a
question this instrument does not answer, and revision 1's "smooth PWM is landable" reading is
**withdrawn** to this approximation pending a measurement that varies the edge continuously.
Square-LFO cells are excluded from improvement claims because their duty jumps at frame boundaries
while the device's jumps wherever its own control path puts it — a timing alignment question between
two step functions, not a smoothing question.

## 5. Directed isolated source negative controls

Four mutations, each applied to a **copy** of the candidate headers inside the throwaway
pinned-baseline worktree, rebuilt, run, and reverted there; the candidate tree's four headers are
hashed before and after and the run fails if they moved. Red requires: builds, probe **exits 0**, and
the **named criterion** fails. A compile error, a dead probe, or a criterion that could not be
evaluated is INVALID and never counted as a hit.

| mutation | what it changes | named criterion | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `bypass` | the correction is computed nowhere | `bypass_must_be_indistinguishable_from_baseline` | **RED** — max \|mutation − baseline\| = **0.0000 dB** over 8 static + 3 dynamic + 2 mixed cells |
| `wrong_sign` | the −2 edge is **added** instead of subtracted | `wrong_sign_must_worsen_vs_baseline` | **RED** — **+2.91..+3.06 dB** vs baseline on all 10 cells |
| `wrong_position` | the moving edge is corrected at `1−duty` | `wrong_position_must_worsen_iff_duty_differs_from_half` | **RED** — **+7.73..+7.92 dB** where `duty ≠ 0.5`, exactly **+0.00** where `duty = 0.5` |
| `pwm_late` | the runtime consumes the **previous frame's** PWM CV | `late_cv_consume_must_break_the_same_frame_reconciliation_on_stepping_cells_only` | **RED** — candidate green **146/146**, static cells inert **8/8**, and the mutant's first unexplained frame is the frame the stimulus predicted, on both judged cells |

**4/4 RED, 0 INVALID, 0 GREEN; the candidate tree's four headers are byte-identical before and
after the run** — their hashes are printed before the run and re-checked at the end.

The `bypass` and `wrong_sign` arms fix the two halves of the claim from opposite sides: `bypass`
reproduces the baseline *exactly*, so the improvement is attributable to the correction and to nothing
else that changed in the candidate; `wrong_sign` keeps a same-shaped two-window correction but flips
one edge's sign, and loses the improvement. The `wrong_position` arm is the one that separates the two
edges: at `duty = 0.5` the wrong position *is* the right position (the two windows swap and the sum is
symmetric), so those cells are inert by construction and the criterion requires worsening only where
`duty ≠ 0.5` — which is what was measured, cell by cell, rather than asserted in aggregate.

### 5.1 `pwm_late`: a real runtime mutation, and a criterion that predicts its own frames

Revision 1's `pwm_late` was wrong in two ways and @Codex said so. It patched the **kernel**, so only
the correction saw a stale duty while the naive pulse and the ring-mix node kept reading the new one —
a self-inconsistent formula rather than a timing error. And it updated **per function call**, which
`sync` may enter several times per frame. Its criterion ("the residual must worsen") had already been
falsified by measurement, and a sensitivity experiment is not a substitute for the product-level
assertion of green-then-red on the same claim.

**The mutation is now the product's own consumption site.** The PWM CV is resolved and consumed in
`core/include/lunar24/core/machine_runtime.h` (`resolveControlSink_(pwmInA_, pwm, driveGraph)` →
`vcA_.setPwCv(pwm)`, and the B mirror), so delaying it there makes the naive pulse, the correction and
the ring-mix node all see the **same** stale duty — exactly one frame late, once per frame.

**The criterion is a stimulus-only frame predicate, declared before the mutant is built.** Against the
recorded source trace and the phase law `align_check` itself uses,

```
P2(cell) = { n in the reconciled window :
               (phi(n) < d[n]) != (phi(n) < d[n-1])
               AND min(|phi|, |phi-1|, |phi-d[n]|, |phi-1+d[n]|) >= dt }
```

A one-frame-late read can only move `align_check`'s load-bearing number on a frame where the stale and
current duties **disagree about the naive sign** and the frame is far enough from every edge to fall
outside the permitted `wrap`/`edge` classes. Both halves are properties of the stimulus alone, so the
set is computable from no rendered sample and no residual. On this grid `P2` is non-empty on exactly
**2 of the 13** slew-qualified cells, and the measurement matches the prediction frame for frame:

```
vco_a_dynpwm_44100_220_sq_r20_b25_d100   RED  unexplained=7  (predicted >=7)  first_bad frame=9922  (predicted 9922, MATCH)
vco_a_dynpwm_96000_220_sq_r20_b25_d100   RED  unexplained=3  (predicted >=3)  first_bad frame=11999 (predicted 11999, MATCH)
candidate arm: 146/146 cells align (unexplained == 0)
mutant arm, static cells: 8/8 still align
```

**The control's coverage boundary is measured and printed, not implied.** The other 11 slew-qualified
cells have `P2` empty — their duty steps land at phases where the two duties agree on the naive sign,
or inside the permitted `edge` class — and the mutant's `unexplained` is **0** on all 11, as
predicted. The runner prints each one with its step count and its measured verdict under a heading
that says they are *reported, not judged*; the residual half of the criterion still covers all 13.
`edge` is a declared blind spot (it is where the candidate's own mismatches live), and this control
hides inside it on most of the grid. The report says that rather than claiming the assertion covers
every stepping cell.

### 5.2 A sign-split finding, kept as a finding

An earlier `pwm_late` criterion predicted the stale duty would make the moving-duty cells **worse**.
That prediction is **falsified by measurement, and there is no clean rule behind the signs.** On the
current build the movement is **+2.80 dB .. -1.39 dB** across the 13 stepping cells, and the split is
**7 cells better, 6 cells worse** — not monotone in LFO rate, in base, or in depth. Grouping by cell,
the two 220 Hz groups move up (`44100`: +0.41/+1.23/-0.39; `96000`: +0.87/+2.80/-0.15) while the
`96000/440` group moves down (-1.39/-1.38/-0.85), so the sign tracks the *oscillator frequency and
rate together*, not the LFO rate alone. Revision 1's "slow-LFO worse, fast-LFO better" reading is
**withdrawn**; the fresh numbers do not support it and the direction is not the claim.

The criterion was restated to what the physics does support — **sensitivity**, not direction — and the
sign split is printed rather than smoothed away. **What this bounds is the dynamic residual's meaning:
in the stepping-duty regime it is not a monotone function of duty-timing error.** The likely cause is
that the reference holds one duty per frame while the product reads the CV per sample, so the
reference's own model error is largest exactly where the duty steps; that mechanism has **not** been
established and nothing here relies on it.

**How the judged cell set was chosen.** My first choice for this criterion was two 2 Hz-LFO cells —
where one frame of duty movement is about 0.02 correction windows, i.e. the *least* sensitive cells
available; the arm was inert there for a reason that had nothing to do with the mutation. The
replacement is the pre-declared stimulus-only rule above. The boundary it exposes is wide: the rule's
threshold is **>= 100 correction windows per frame** and only 13 of the 56 moving-duty cells reach it;
the smooth-PWM (triangle-LFO) cells the report claims improvement on sit far below it, at or under
**0.182 windows/frame**, while square-LFO cells reach **326.8**. The runner prints the 13 qualifying
cells with their rates, and the smooth-PWM cells it reports (rather than judges) with theirs, so the
gap is auditable rather than asserted; the un-referenced moving-duty cells are censused separately in
the delta matrix's per-block reconciliation (§10, finding 3).

## 9. What this does not claim

* Not a band-limited or exact pulse. It removes the alias energy the naive step produces; the total
  residual falls on every measured cell, but the floor/harmonic split is a shape column and cannot
  identify aliases (§2.1).
* Not a claim that the dynamic residual is a validated metric for duty-timing error (§5.2).
* Not a claim that a smoothly-moving PWM edge is landable; the dynamic column is a
  frozen-duty-per-frame approximation (§4.2).
* Not a resolution of the correction's per-frame cost (§6).
* Not a claim about MSVC: this machine has Apple clang only, so all of the above is a local
  macOS/clang result. **Local macOS/clang green is not MSVC green.**
* Not a production acceptance target. No global -60 dB is pre-set, no existing threshold was changed,
  and GH #19 / #15 remain open with no MET judgement.
* **Not a claim that the correction is valid across the pitch box the frozen registry declares**
  (§11.3). The kernel's proven domain is `dt ≤ 0.5` and the registered box reaches `dt ≈ 1308`; the
  instrument leaves the proven domain at an ordinary 1.647 V at the V/OCT jack with the panel
  octave/tune at maximum. What is measured past that point is what the formula *does*, not that it is
  correct, and §11.3 states the boundary as broken rather than merely unverified.
* **Not a design decision on what to do about it.** The high-step strategies in §11.3 are candidate
  list and cost, handed to @Codex; nothing here changes the frequency/CV law. **That item has since
  been decided by @Codex and implemented in revision 4** (§12), and the decision is narrower than
  §11.3.4's list in exactly the way that keeps this bullet true: it caps the width of the correction
  kernel and leaves the frequency, the CV law, the duty and the PWM transfer untouched.
* **Not, out of domain, an anti-aliasing improvement — revision 4 says so in the header and here.**
  Past `dt = 0.5` the capped correction is a **bounded continuous waveform extension**, and it is
  explicitly *not* the correct correction for that step width, *not* a super-Nyquist improvement, and
  *not* a hard switch back to the naive pulse. At `dt >= 1` the phase advances a full cycle or more
  per sample and the naive pulse is undersampled, so no correction can band-limit a waveform that is
  not being sampled; what is claimed there is boundedness and continuity, and nothing else (§12.1).
* The two out-of-domain sweep columns for `dt ≥ 1` are **not** a statement about a band-limited
  pulse: at `dt ≥ 1` the phase advances a full cycle or more per sample and the naive pulse is
  undersampled, so the closed form quoted for `0.5 < dt < 1` does not apply there (`n/a` in the
  grid). Boundedness is reported there; correctness is not claimed.

### 11.2 Item 2 — the 100–5000 Hz / effective-band columns are output residuals, not kernel gains

The previous revision's matrix carried only the full-window `res_db`. The contract asks for the
**output residual** in a band, so each cell now carries two more columns, measured on the **output**
`x` against the reference `A·r`:

```
band_residual_db(x, r, A, sr, f_lo, f_hi) = 10*log10( band_power(x − A·r) / band_power(A·r) )
```

with **one** declared denominator, a Hann window power-normalized by `sum(w²)`, and the reference's own
DC/Nyquist half-weighting. They are not the decimator's passband flatness — that is a *precondition*,
asserted separately in `--self-check (d)` — and each matrix's own `BAND-DECL` line states the bands so
they cannot drift between arms:

| block | b1_lo / b1_hi | effective band | rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| static | 100 / 5000 Hz | `[0, sr/2]` | `output_nyquist` — the analytic reference is trustworthy over `(0, sr/2]` exactly, because the series is truncated at the last harmonic under Nyquist |
| mixed | 100 / 5000 Hz | `[0, sr/2]` | `output_nyquist`, against this block's own mixed reference (comparable within the block, not across the fence) |
| dynamic | 100 / 5000 Hz | `[0, 0.483750·sr]` | `decimator_0.1db` — the 0.1 dB endpoint of the **measured** decimator response; `edge_L = 0.491875` |

The 100–5000 Hz column needs no truncation only if the narrowest effective band contains 5000 Hz:
21333 Hz (@44100) > 5000 Hz, so the two columns are consistent on this grid, and that is recomputed
rather than assumed.

**The two columns are different measurements, and the numbers say so.** Over the 94 compared cells,
`|res_effbd_db − res_db| ≤ 0.35 dB` (base arm worst −0.27 dB; candidate arm worst +0.35 dB), which is
why the per-column improvement slack is 1.0 dB. `|res_1k5k_db − res_db|` instead reaches **24.18 dB**
on the candidate arm — i.e. the band column is not a restatement of the total.

**Improvements, negative = better, one column per residual, so the columns are read separately:**

| block | cells | `res_db` | `res_1k5k_db` | `res_effbd_db` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| static | 80 | −9.50 … −4.71 (median −7.55) | −28.33 … −8.53 (median −20.37) | −9.50 … −4.71 (median −7.53) |
| dynamic | 8 | −7.48 … −6.77 (median −7.36) | −15.09 … −12.02 (median −14.16) | −7.59 … −6.88 (median −7.31) |
| mixed | 6 | −7.54 … −7.49 | −21.15 … −15.86 | −7.55 … −7.46 |

Every cell improves in **all three** columns (80/80, 8/8, 6/6, each > 0.50 dB). The `res_db` and
`res_effbd_db` columns move together because they share a band on this grid; the `res_1k5k` column
moves by roughly 2–3× more, which is the expected shape (the correction's effect is concentrated where
the step's images are) and is reported as a **finding**, not as a second independent confirmation.

**Band-column self-check, kept separate from the improvement claim.** A band column that moved in the
same direction as `res_db` for the wrong reason would be worse than no column:

### 11.3 Item 3 — the real large-step domain, the reachable box, and the hand-over

#### 11.3.1 What `pulseBlepCorr_` actually does with a large step

> **SUPERSEDED IN PART BY §12.** This section describes the kernel as it stood at revision 3 and is
> kept as the *diagnosis* that motivated the ruling, not as a description of the current tree. The
> statement below that the step reaches the kernel with "no upper bound" **is no longer true**: since
> revision 4 the width handed to the residual is capped at `kPolyblepMaxDt`, while the phase still
> advances by the true step (§12.1). What is unchanged is the *finding* — the three regimes, the
> residual's own step at `t = dt`, and the measured reachable box are all exactly as measured here,
> and §12 relies on them.

`pulseBlepCorr_` (`vco.h`) passes the step straight to the kernel with only
`if (!(dt > 0.0)) return 0.0;` — no upper bound, unlike the triangle path, which does
`dt = |step|`, guards `!(dt>0)`/`!isfinite(dt)` and then **`if (blampSupportReachesHalfPeriod(dt))
return 0.0;`** and sums every corner inside the window. The asymmetry is real and the review is right
that it is unstated. What the kernel does with a large step has **three regimes**, and the review's
`dt = 0.75` coordinate sits in the second:

| regime | what happens | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `0 < dt ≤ 0.5` | both residual windows fit inside one period; `R` is continuous. **The proven domain**, and `kPolyblepMaxDt = 0.5` is exactly its edge | `1 − dt ≥ dt` |
| `0.5 < dt < 1` | `1 − dt < dt`, so branch 1 covers `[0, dt)` and branch 2 covers `[dt, 1)`. `R(dt⁻) = 0`, `R(dt⁺) = (2 − 1/dt)²`: **the residual itself steps.** `naive + correction` therefore carries an extra jump of that size at `t = dt` and a second, opposite-sign one at `t = duty + dt` (when `duty + dt < 1`). The step grows from **0 at `dt = 0.5`** to **1 at `dt = 1`** — so the contract boundary is the *last continuous point*, not the first broken one | `t < dt` and `t > 1 − dt` both fire, in that order |
| `dt ≥ 1` | `t < dt` for every `t ∈ [0,1)`: branch 1 everywhere, and the phase advances a full cycle or more per output sample. A two-point step correction has no referent — the naive pulse is undersampled | `dt ≥ 1` |

**Measured, not derived** (`kernel_sweep.txt`, all of it reproduced by the negative controls below):

* **The review's coordinate reproduces to 15 digits.** `dt = 0.75`, `duty = 0.2`,
  `t = 0.75 ∓ 1e-10` → **−0.928888888817778 / −0.484444444337778**, exactly the two numbers @Codex
  printed. `R(t) = 0` below and `0.444444444622222` above; jump across `t = 0.75` =
  `0.444444444480000` against the closed form `(2 − 1/0.75)² = 0.444444444444445`; and the second extra
  jump at `t = duty + dt = 0.95` measures `0.444444444124444` with the opposite sign.
* **The jump is the residual's, at every `dt` in `(0.5, 1)`.** 11 values; the one-sided difference
  converges to `(2 − 1/dt)²` as ε falls (ε = 1e-6 / 1e-9 / 1e-12), and the `dt = 0.5` row's limit is
  `0` against a closed form of `0`. The three columns show the **limit** — a one-sided difference of a
  jump plus a finite slope is *not* ε-invariant, and an earlier draft of the driver asserted that it
  was and went red on all 11 rows.
* **Jump versus slope is decided by resolution, not by the one-sided difference.** The t-gap of
  `dt = 0.5` **falls 1000×** (`0.0016 → 0.0000016`) as the t-grid goes `1e3 → 1e6` samples, i.e. it is
  continuous; the t-gaps of `0.6, 2/3, 0.75, 0.9, 0.999` are **flat** (`0.1100000 → 0.1111100`) and
  match `(2 − 1/dt)²` to <1e-3, i.e. a genuine jump. The contract boundary is therefore verified as
  the boundary, from the same grid that reports the break past it.
* **Boundedness survives out of domain; continuity does not.** `max|out| ≤ 1` everywhere measured over
  `duty ∈ [1e-3, 1−1e-3]`, worst excess **−1.50e-6**, and that holds at `dt ≥ 1` too
  (`dt = 13 → −1.42e-4`). These are two different claims, and only the second one is broken. The
  previous revision's "verified bounded and continuous" is **not** carried past the contract.

#### 11.3.3 Stop, reverse, and large steps — the strategy, and what is actually reachable

* **`dt ≤ 0` is handled, and handled exactly.** Over 7 non-positive values (`0`, `−0`, `−1e-300`,

  for bit rather than to a tolerance.
* **The product cannot produce a negative step at all.** `frequencyHz()` clamps (`return p > 0.0 ? p :

  reverse: measured, CV lin −2 V gives `frequencyHz() = 0` exactly, and `baseHz = 0` driven through the
  real `Vco::tick()` for 64 samples emits a constant with **deviation 0.000000000000000** (the naive
  pulse at the held phase, correction exactly 0). There is no reverse execution strategy to specify
  because there is no reverse, and `dt = |step|` (C3d) would only matter if FM or reversal were ever
  wired here — they are not.
* **Large steps: bounded, but not continuous, and outside the kernel's stated contract.** The regime
  table and the measurements above are the whole of what is claimed. The kernel is a documented

  "verified bounded and continuous" must not be carried across the boundary. It is now split into

## 12. Revision 4 — the width cap

**This is the first revision that changes what the kernel DOES outside `dt <= 0.5`**, and it is the
only revision here that was ordered as a ruling rather than requested as a review. @Codex's direction
(msg `4940d6ea`, answering the hand-over in §11.3.4):

> 有限正 step 只将**修正核宽度**设为 `w = min(step, 0.5)`;非正 step 保持修正 0。实际相位仍按原 step
> 推进,不改频率/CV law、duty 或 PWM 传递。超 0.5 延用边界核,只称有界连续的波形延拓,不称超
> Nyquist 抗混叠改善;不再硬切回 naive。

That is the fourth option §11.3.4's table did not name. It is **not C3b** (bound the pitch at the
source — a frequency/CV-law change, which the review reserved and this revision still does not make)
and **not C3a** (fall back to the naive pulse out of domain — the rejected candidate, now a live RED
control in §12.3 rather than a proposal).

### 12.1 The change, and the three regimes

```cpp
inline double polyblepPulseCorrection(double t, double duty, double dt) {
  if (!(dt > 0.0)) return 0.0;
  const double w = (dt < kPolyblepMaxDt) ? dt : kPolyblepMaxDt;   // <- the entire change
  double b = t - duty;
  b -= std::floor(b);                                             // frac: B's window is centred on B
  return polyblepResidual(t, w) - polyblepResidual(b, w);
}
```

**The phase accumulator is not touched.** The oscillator still advances by the true step, and the
frequency, the CV law, the duty and the PWM transfer are exactly what they were. The only thing that
changed is the width the residual is evaluated at. `kPolyblepMaxDt` is `0.5` and is reported by the
sweep (`BOX kPolyblepMaxDt=0.500000`), so the constant in the code is the constant in the evidence.

| regime | what `w` is | what is claimed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `0 < dt <= 0.5` | `w = dt`, and `min(dt, 0.5)` **is** `dt`, so the ternary is an identity | **Bit-identical to the pre-cap header.** Asserted, not assumed: 3,606,000 in-domain samples, **0 bit-differences** (`OVER-STRUCT-CAPISIDENTITY`) |
| `0.5 < dt < 1` | `w = 0.5`, held while the phase advances by the true `dt` | The emitted waveform is **continuous** here, where the uncapped residual stepped by `(2 - 1/dt)^2` at `t = dt` | 
| `dt >= 1` | `w = 0.5` | **Bounded and continuous.** The phase advances a full cycle or more per sample, so the naive pulse is undersampled and no band-limited representation of the intended pulse exists to approximate. Boundedness and continuity are claimed; correctness is not |

Out of domain the correction is **still applied** — with a fixed width, not switched off. The
distinction is the whole content of the ruling, and the sweep measures it as a number rather than as
a description: at `t = 0.75`, `dt = 0.75`, the two-sided difference at `eps = 1e-10` is
`3.20000026476919e-10` under the cap against a **jump of 0.444444444444445** uncapped
(`OVER-REPRO-CAP` / `OVER-RESIDUAL-UNC 0.75  +4.444444442e-01 +4.444444444e-01`). The rejected
C3a arm, for contrast, is exactly the naive pulse at that coordinate (`OVER-REPRO-GUARDED t=0.75

the bisection and the law are the same object. The declared box maxima (`CV exp +5V -> dt 1307.75`,
`CV lin +5V -> dt 245.20`) are *outside* the kernel contract and are reported as such.

The crossover is then measured **on the object, across the boundary**, which is where the rejected
candidate would show up:

```

```

The largest step over the whole 2e-7-wide window is `4.98e-07`, i.e. `~2*d` — the turn-on is
continuous. **The same grid step under the rejected C3a arm measures 1.81**, which is the hard
discontinuity at the switch point; that number is in §12.3, not here, and the pair is the comparison
the ruling rests on. The far side of the boundary is additionally checked sample-for-sample against
`out(dt = cap)`, so "the cap was applied" is not inferred from the cap state alone.

**12.2.3 Stop and reverse.**

```
VCO-STOP lin CV -1.0V (bracket exactly 0)   freq=0  step=0  samples!=first=0  range=[1.000000000000000, 1.000000000000000]  phase=0
VCO-STOP lin CV -5.0V (bracket negative)    freq=0  step=0  samples!=first=0  range=[1.000000000000000, 1.000000000000000]  phase=0
VCO-STOP exp V/OCT -1200V (underflow)       freq=0  step=0  samples!=first=0  range=[1.000000000000000, 1.000000000000000]  phase=0
VCO-STOP negative_pitch_returns=0
BOX stop: baseHz=0 over 64 ticks -> first=1.000000000000000  max deviation=0.000000000000000  (naive pulse at the held phase, correction exactly 0)
```

Three ways to reach `step = 0` — exactly-zero bracket, negative bracket, and exponential underflow —
and all three render the naive pulse at the held phase with the correction exactly zero, over 64
ticks. The `dt <= 0` exit is unchanged and is what this rests on. The last line is the honest
bound on the reverse question: the product has **no** path to a negative `step` (`frequencyHz()`
clamps before the kernel sees it), so the kernel's `dt < 0` branch is unreachable from the product
rather than exercised by it. That is a statement about the caller, not a claim that reversal works.

**12.2.4 Block processing and sync.**

```
VCO-WIRING samples=40960 bit-mismatches=0 worst_abs=0 sup|out|=1.000000000000000  (tick_out == polyblepPulse(phase(), effectiveDuty(), step))
VCO-BLOCK samples=4096 bit-different=0  (1 x 4096 vs 64 x 64, voct=1.7 => dt beyond the cap)
VCO-SYNC resets=201  phase_after_reset_nonzero=0  formula_mismatches=0  sup|out|=0.500000000000000
```

`VCO-WIRING` is the identity that makes the sweep's claims claims about the product: the object's
emitted sample equals the kernel formula evaluated at the object's own phase and duty, bit for bit,
over 40960 samples. `VCO-BLOCK` is run with `voct = 1.7` — **past the cap** — and compares one
4096-sample block against 64 blocks of 64, bit for bit; a per-call correction that depended on call
boundaries would show up here. `VCO-SYNC` resets the phase 201 times and checks the post-reset sample
against the formula each time, with `sup|out| = 0.5` on the sync path.

### 12.4 Two corrections to my own instrument, forced by these controls

Both are recorded because they were found by a control failing to behave, not by review — and in the
first case the control that found it was the **positive** control, i.e. the failure mode was in the
tool, not in the candidate.

**12.4.1 A positive control that an unrelated reporting function could switch off.** The sweep's
positive control is the refuted revision's 0.5625 jump, and reconstructing it means reconstructing
the refuted predicate. That reconstruction was reading the **live** `polyblepPulseWindowsDisjoint` —
a function the current header declares to be reporting-only. The `label_drift` control drifted that
predicate by 0.01 and **killed the positive control**, which the runner correctly reports as "the
driver can no longer see the refutation" and propagates as INVALID. A control that a change to an
unrelated reporting function can switch off is not an independent control. It is now frozen in the
driver as `refutedRevisionWindowsDisjoint`, transcribed from `58f41be`, so it is a fixed fact about
that revision; a drift in the shipped predicate is measured on its own terms by `OVER-STRUCT-LABEL`.
The header's reporting-only claim and the tool's independence were, in effect, competing, and the fix
is to stop the tool from depending on the thing under test.

**12.4.2 A prediction of mine that was wrong, and the check that was measuring nothing.** When
`c3a_fallback` was written I predicted it would redden `OVER-STRUCT-EXTENSION` ("`out(dt)` IS
`out(0.5)`"). **It does not.** The inserted guard is also true **at** `dt = 0.5`, so *both* sides of
that identity collapse to the naive pulse and the check passes **trivially**. An identity that two
sides can satisfy by both being degenerate is not measuring the extension. The prediction is kept as
a comment in the runner rather than deleted, and the criterion is now the t-gap discriminator, which
does see the defect. This is the second time in this slice that a check passed for a reason other than
the claim it stood for, and both times the control that caught it was a control that was *supposed*
to go red and didn't.

### 12.5 What revision 4 does not claim

* **Not** a super-Nyquist anti-aliasing improvement. Past `dt = 0.5` the cap is a bounded continuous
  waveform **extension**; no reduction in alias energy is claimed, measured, or implied there.
* **Not** the correct correction for those step widths. It is the boundary kernel, held.
* **Not** a hard switch back to the naive pulse: the correction term is still evaluated out of
  domain. `OVER-REPRO-GUARDED` is what switching off would look like, and it is the rejected arm.
* **Not** a change to anything in `dt <= 0.5`, and therefore not a change to any number in §2, §4 or
  §6 of this report. The in-domain identity is asserted at the sample level rather than argued.
* **Not** a resolution of `dt >= 1`. There the phase advances a full cycle or more per sample and the
  naive pulse is undersampled; the cap makes the output bounded and continuous, and that is the end
  of what is claimed.
* **Not** pushed, merged, or accepted. `ee1f5f5` and `a839b5d` stay frozen; this revision stacks on
  them. **GH #19 / #15 remain open, nothing was released, and no MET judgement is made.**

### 12.6 The two small fixes, and where they live

Both are in `§11.2`, and both are revision-4 work even though the section they are written up in is
revision 3's — they were directed against §11.2 and the edits are there.

1. **The missing one-sided `2/N` coefficient.** `band_residual_db` returned `pe`/`pq` without the
   one-sided coefficient, so the *ratio* — and every dB column — was right while the *units* were not
   mean squares. This is exactly the failure mode where a correct-looking number hides an incorrect
   quantity. Fixed, and then pinned by a **Parseval reconciliation in absolute units** rather than by
   a second ratio: the new `(e) Parseval absolute` self-check compares the returned `pe`/`pq` against
   direct windowed mean squares `sum (w*y)^2 / sum w^2`, on both a Hann and a rect window. Measured
   agreement is ~1e-16 relative on the baseline arm.
2. **`2L` is a reference change, not "reference error removed".** The two columns are now reported
   separately and are of different sizes: **1.11 / 1.70 / 1.96 dB** across the 100–5000 Hz column,
   **0.13 / 0.22 / 0.22 dB** for the effective full band. The review's `0.97–1.96 dB` range was
   **my own error quoted back to me** — `0.97` is not the difference between any two of those
   numbers, and the paragraph that produced it also mislabelled the 100–5000 Hz column as "the
   effective band". Corrected in §11.2, with the correction recorded. The mechanism is reported as
   **observed, not explained**.

The new self-check has its own negative control: a mutated copy of the analyzer with the `2/N`
coefficient removed leaves the dB ratio **byte-identical** (9.81e-13, unchanged) while the new
absolute lines go red and the rest of the 152-cell matrix does not move. That is the point — the
check that was added can see the thing the old check could not.
